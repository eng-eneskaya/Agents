# Audience

## Who They Are
Global YouTube viewers curious about what different jobs actually look, sound, and pay like — drawn to immersive, ASMR-style work footage and the satisfaction of watching real, skilled labor up close.

## Pain Points
- Generic "day in the life" content rarely gives a straight answer on what a job actually pays or requires
- Career-curious viewers weighing options (trades vs. white-collar) want an honest, first-person look before they'd ever consider it themselves
- Most work-related content is either narrated lecture-style or badly shot — viewers want calming, high-quality ambient footage without needing to fully focus on dialogue

## Goals
- Vicarious experience of jobs they'll likely never do themselves
- Quick, concrete answers: pay, tools used, what the day-to-day is really like
- Relaxation/ASMR value from the ambient sounds of real work (metal, water, tools, materials)

## Language
- Curiosity-driven, direct phrasing: "How much does a [job] actually make?", "What's it like to be a [job] for a day?"
- On-screen text/captions carry the informational payload, since dialogue is often minimal or absent in the ASMR-style cuts

## Segments

### Segment 1: ASMR / immersive-work viewers
- Who: Watch for the sound and visual satisfaction of real manual work (blacksmith, fisherman, cleaner)
- Pain: Want calming, high-quality ambient footage without narration getting in the way
- Goal: Relaxation and sensory immersion

### Segment 2: Career-curious / salary-curious viewers
- Who: Watch higher-skill profession episodes (doctor, developer) for real talk on pay and tools
- Pain: Career content online is often vague about actual income and day-to-day tools
- Goal: Concrete, honest information to inform their own career thinking

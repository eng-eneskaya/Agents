## Patterns Noticed
<!-- Emerging signals that need more data to confirm. -->

- No direct Turkish-market equivalent of this format exists (checked ~12 Turkish search queries: "meslek deneme kanalı", "bir günlüğüne işçi oldum", "iş asmr türkçe", etc.) — treat the first local (Turkey) episodes as a genuine format test, not something benchmarked against an existing local audience baseline. (Source: 2026-08-20 competitor analysis, `outputs/2026-08-20_youtube_competitor-analysis.md`)
- Pure ASMR-only channels with no captions/context (e.g. Kiwami Japan, ~2.7M subs) draw comments about viewers losing track of what's happening — supports keeping our caption-driven pay/tool info layer rather than going fully silent.
- Pure fact/salary-reveal channels with no sensory layer (e.g. CNBC Make It) don't combine ASMR immersion with hard pay/tool facts — no competitor found currently does both, which is our core differentiation bet and should be protected in editing (don't drop captions in favor of "cleaner" ASMR cuts, and don't drop the ASMR pacing in favor of a more narrated explainer style).
- Comments on trying-professions / primitive-skill channels frequently raise "is this staged or edited?" suspicion (notably around Primitive Technology imitator channels) — footage should visibly preserve unedited struggle/failure moments rather than cutting to only the polished results, to preserve trust.
- Viewers of this content type repeatedly want "the training/effort behind the number," not just the salary figure itself — captions should pair each pay fact with what it took to get there (years of training, licensing, apprenticeship), not the number alone.
- BBC's "Toughest Place to Be a…" format (a professional doing their own job abroad under much harder conditions) is a close structural precedent for our planned international episodes (e.g. the Japan katana-apprenticeship idea) — worth reviewing an episode of it before scripting that arc.
